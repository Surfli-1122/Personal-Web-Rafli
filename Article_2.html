What They Don't Tell You About Life
in an Islamic Boarding School

School years always have a unique way of staying with us, but growing up inside a boarding
school hits a whole different level. During my three years at Ar-Rohmah Putra Islamic Boarding
School in Malang, I didn't just learn about life in theory-I lived it through tight discipline, real
brotherhood, and all the moments that shaped who I am today.

If I'm being completely honest, my journey there was far from the model-student path. Among
all the memories I made, there's a whole chapter dedicated to breaking the rules. My personal
record-and a pretty bold one at that-was slipping out of the compound without permission
around 30 to 40 times over those three years. Those sneaky escapes weren't just about
rebelling they were my way of catching a breath, chasing a bit of an adrenaline rush, and
figuring out what freedom felt like within a strict setup. Every escape had its own story, and
every time I snuck back in, I had to be ready to face the music.

Yet, Ar-Rohmah wasn't just a backdrop for my wilder side. It was also the place that built my
mindset and expanded my horizons.

That’s where my understanding of Islam grew deeper-moving beyond just memorization into
real-life values and perspective. It’s also where my love for languages took root. Alongside
sharpening my English, the boarding school became the starting point for my interest in
German. I started getting fascinated by its sentence structure, its distinct writing vibe, and even
tried putting together German-style texts in my spare time. It was a cool blend of traditional
boarding school life and a curiosity for the wider world.

Those three years in Malang were never just about counting down the days until graduation.
They were about real friendships, intense life lessons, pushed boundaries, and knowledge that
actually stuck.

Every bit of it—the laughs, the reckless moments, and the process of growing up—carved out a
story I'm proud of. These memories will never be forgotten.
